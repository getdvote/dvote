What changed in v2.0. The business moved from "buy N coffees, get 1 free" with NFC cards to spend-based points: each vendor sets how much money earns a point (e.g. 10 EGP = 1 point) and publishes a catalogue of rewards priced in points (e.g. free coffee 300 pts). Points are collected and redeemed with one-time QR codes shown in the customer app and scanned by vendor staff in a new staff app. Removed: nfc_tags, NTAG 424 security, the 10-minute cooldown, programs, wallets, scan_sessions. New: point_rules, rewards, cards, qr_codes. Login moved to Supabase Auth (built in v1.2 work). Items new in this version are marked NEW; endpoints already implemented are marked BUILT.

SECTION 1

Introduction

What the platform is, who uses it and the rules it follows.

1.1 Overview

A multi-vendor loyalty platform for coffee shops. Customers earn points on every purchase, in proportion to what they spend, and spend those points on rewards that each vendor defines. Points belong to one vendor: points earned at Ecuador can only be spent at Ecuador.

Vendor dashboard (web)Vendor admins, branch managersBranches, staff, point rule, reward catalogue, reports
Admin dashboard (web)Platform teamAll vendors, onboarding, customer support, point corrections, fraud review

Rewards are paid for by the vendor. For the vendor, the platform is a customer-retention and advertising channel.

v1 scope: collect points by QR, redeem rewards by QR, per-vendor cards, the staff app and both dashboards. Point expiry and push notifications are deferred (see Roadmap).

1.3 Core concepts and business rules

Vendor vs branch. A vendor is the brand (e.g. Ecuador, Joy Corner); a branch is one physical shop. A chain with 6 shops = 1 vendor, 6 branches.

Card. One card per customer per vendor: the customer's points at that vendor. A card is created automatically on the customer's first purchase at that vendor; a customer with no points at a vendor has no card for it. Points from any branch of the same vendor add to the same card and never move between vendors.

Point rule NEW. Each vendor has one active rule (its "settings"): every spend_amount spent earns points_per_spend points, e.g. every 10 EGP = 1 point. Optional: a minimum purchase and a maximum number of points per receipt. Points are always rounded down: 95 EGP at 10 EGP/point = 9 points. Rules are versioned: a new rule applies to purchases from then on; past points never change.

Rewards NEW. Each vendor publishes a catalogue of rewards priced in points, e.g. free coffee 300 · free cheesecake 500 · coffee + cheesecake 750. Rewards can be edited or archived, never deleted; a redemption keeps a copy of the reward's name and price at that moment.

Collecting. The customer shows a QR; staff scan it and enter the receipt total; the server applies the vendor's rule and adds the points. The app never decides points.

Redeeming. The customer picks a reward they can afford and shows a redeem QR; staff scan it; the server checks the balance, deducts the reward's points and records the redemption. The customer chooses when to redeem; nothing is automatic. Leftover points stay on the card (1000 − 300 = 700).

One-time QR codes NEW. Every QR holds a random single-use code issued by the server, valid for 5 minutes. A screenshot or photo of an old QR is useless.

Ledger is the source of truth. A card's balance is a cached total and must always equal the sum of that card's point events (earns positive, redemptions negative, admin adjustments either). Every balance change is written in the same database transaction as its ledger row.

Tenant scoping. Staff only ever see and act on their own vendor (and their own branch, for branch roles).

SECTION 3

Flows and Example

How collecting and redeeming work, then a walk-through with a real vendor.

3.1 Collect flow (earning points)

There are two ways to show a collect QR; the server handles both the same way.

QRWhere in the appContainsVendor comes from
Master QRVendors list pageOne-time code → customerThe staff member who scans it
Vendor QRA vendor's pageOne-time code → customer + that vendorThe QR; other vendors' staff are refused (vendor_mismatch)

In both cases the branch comes from the scanning staff member, never from the customer's phone. A vendor admin (who has no branch) picks the branch in the staff app.

  1. Customer taps Collect points (master or vendor QR). The app calls POST /api/app/qr-codes {purpose:"collect", vendorId?}; the server stores a qr_codes row (status active, expires in 5 min, only the code's hash is stored) and returns the code. Any older active collect QR of that customer is cancelled.
  2. The app shows the QR and waits, checking the QR's status every 2 seconds.
  3. Staff scan it in the staff app. The app calls POST /api/vendor/scans/preview to show what the QR is (collect; vendor allowed).
  4. Staff type the receipt total (and optionally the receipt number) and submit POST /api/vendor/scans/collect {code, amount, receiptRef?, branchId?, idempotencyKey}.
  5. The server runs the checks below, computes the points with the vendor's active rule and, in one transaction: creates the card if this is the customer's first purchase at this vendor, inserts a point_events row (earn, +points, amount, rule version, branch, staff), adds the points to cards.balance and lifetime_points, and marks the QR used.
  6. The staff app shows "+9 points added"; the customer app sees the QR is used and shows "+9 points at Joy Corner · balance 129".

Server checks for collect, in order

  1. Code exists and is active and not expired (qr_invalid, qr_used, qr_expired).
  2. Idempotency key already used → return the original result, add nothing.
  3. QR is a collect QR (wrong_qr_type).
  4. Staff active; branch known (branch_required for vendor admins); vendor and branch active.
  5. Vendor QR: its vendor = the staff member's vendor (vendor_mismatch).
  6. Customer not blocked (user_blocked).
  7. Vendor has an active rule (no_active_rule); amount valid (invalid_amount); points > 0 (no_points_earned, e.g. 8 EGP at 10 EGP/point or below the minimum purchase); receipt number not already used at this branch (duplicate_receipt).

A failed check returns a clear error to the staff app and changes nothing; the QR stays usable so staff can correct a typo.

3.2 Redeem flow

  1. On a vendor's page, the app lists the rewards with their prices and marks the ones the card can afford.
  2. Customer taps a reward → POST /api/app/qr-codes {purpose:"redeem", rewardId}. The server checks the reward is active and the balance is enough (so the customer gets an early "not enough points"), then issues a redeem QR tied to that vendor and reward.
  3. Staff scan it → preview shows "Redeem: Free coffee · 300 pts" so staff know what to serve.
  4. Staff confirm → POST /api/vendor/scans/redeem {code, branchId?, idempotencyKey}.
  5. Checks: code valid (as above); it is a redeem QR; its vendor = the staff member's vendor (vendor_mismatch); reward still active (reward_unavailable); customer not blocked; balance still enough (insufficient_points).
  6. One transaction: 
    UPDATE cards SET balance = balance - $cost
WHERE id = $card AND balance >= $cost
RETURNING balance;            -- no row ⇒ not enough points ⇒ abort
    then insert point_events (redeem, −cost, reward, branch, staff), insert redemptions (with the reward's name and cost copied), mark the QR used.
  7. Both apps show "Reward approved: Free coffee", branch and time; staff serve the reward.

3.3 What the customer sees

  • My cards: one card per vendor where the customer has points (none at first). Each card shows the vendor, balance, and progress to the next reward ("120 / 300 to Free coffee").
  • Vendors: all active vendors with their rule ("10 EGP = 1 point") and the master Collect points QR button.
  • Vendor page: branches, rule, rewards (affordable ones highlighted), the vendor Collect points QR and a Redeem button per reward.
  • History: every earn, redemption and correction on a card, with date, branch and amount.

4.3 Constraints and indexes

  • One active rule per vendor (partial unique index); UNIQUE (vendor_id, version).
  • cards: one per (user, vendor); balance ≥ 0.
  • point_events: earn ⇒ delta > 0 with amount, rule, QR, staff, branch; redeem ⇒ delta < 0 with reward, QR, staff, branch; adjust ⇒ admin and reason. UPDATE and DELETE are blocked by a trigger: corrections are new adjust rows.
  • Composite foreign keys on (…, vendor_id): a ledger row's card, branch, rule and reward must all belong to its vendor, so points can never cross vendors even by a bug.
  • At most one ledger row per QR code; a receipt number earns once per branch.
  • qr_codes: redeem ⇒ vendor and reward set; collect ⇒ no reward; used ⇒ used_at, staff and branch set.
  • Indexes: point_events by (card, time), (branch, time), (vendor, time), (staff, time); rewards by (vendor, status, order); qr_codes by (user, time); fraud_flags by (status, time).

5.2 Supporting services

  • Supabase Auth: customers sign in with Google or Facebook; staff are invited by email and set a password. Supabase issues and refreshes tokens; the backend checks them locally with the project's public keys and maps each one to its own users or staff_users row. Apple sign-in is postponed (paid developer account); an iOS release offering Google/Facebook will likely need it.
  • Email: Supabase sends staff invites and password resets; production needs its own SMTP provider (the built-in sender is rate-limited).
  • Push notifications (later): Firebase Cloud Messaging, to confirm points when the app is closed.
  • Hosting: one backend container plus one managed PostgreSQL. No microservices, queues or Redis in v1.

5.3 Backend decisions

  • Balance changes are atomic. Earn and redeem run inside prisma.$transaction with the ledger insert. Redemption uses a conditional UPDATE … WHERE balance >= cost; marking the QR used is also conditional (WHERE status = 'active'), so the same QR can never be processed twice, even by two staff phones at once.
  • Money is exact. Amounts are numeric(12,2) in the database and integer minor units (piastres) in code, never floating point. Points round down.
  • Tenant scoping in one place. A guard builds the staff member's context (vendor, branch, role) once per request; every vendor-side query filters by it.
  • Idempotency. The staff app sends a fresh key with each submit; a retry after a lost response returns the original result.
  • Customer confirmation. While a QR is on screen the customer app checks its status every 2 seconds and shows the result as soon as it is used.
  • Fraud checks: a scheduled job writes fraud_flags; flags never block a purchase by themselves.
  • Modules: auth, users, vendors, branches, staff, point-rules, rewards, cards, qr-codes, scans (collect + redeem), redemptions, fraud-flags, admin, reports.

5.4 QR code security NEW

The QR never contains the customer's id or anything personal: it holds a random one-time code (128 bits) issued by the server, e.g. dvote:q1:Zx3….

ThreatProtection
Screenshot or photo of a QR reused laterSingle use; expires after 5 minutes; a new QR cancels the previous one
Someone guesses another customer's QR128-bit random code; nothing to guess from a user id
Database leak exposes live QR codesOnly the SHA-256 hash is stored
Staff of another vendor redeem a rewardRedeem QRs are tied to one vendor and reward (vendor_mismatch)
Same QR scanned on two staff phones at onceConditional update: only the first succeeds
Points awarded twice for one receiptOne ledger row per QR; optional receipt number unique per branch

The app never decides points: it only shows the code. All API calls require a signed-in token over HTTPS.

8.2 Automatic fraud flags

A scheduled job writes one fraud_flags row per finding, with the numbers in details. Flags never block anything on their own; a platform admin reviews them.

  • too_many_collects: more than 3 collects for one customer in a day at one vendor.
  • large_purchase: a receipt far above the branch's usual size (e.g. 5× its 30-day average).
  • branch_spike: a branch's daily points above 3× its 30-day average.
  • staff_spike: one staff member's daily points above 3× their own average.

SECTION 9

Decisions and Roadmap

What is decided, and what comes later.

9.1 Decisions

  • Decided NEW: spend-based points with a versioned rule per vendor; points round down.
  • Decided NEW: each vendor defines a reward catalogue priced in points; the customer chooses when to redeem.
  • Decided NEW: QR codes replace NFC. QRs hold a one-time server-issued code (5 minutes, single use), never the user id.
  • Decided NEW: master QR (any vendor) and vendor QR (one vendor); the branch always comes from the scanning staff member.
  • Decided NEW: a separate staff app (React Native) for scanning.
  • Decided: Supabase Auth: Google and Facebook for customers, invited email + password for staff; Apple postponed.
  • Decided: React Native + Expo for the mobile apps (replaces Flutter).
  • Removed: NFC cards, NTAG 424 DNA, the 10-minute cooldown (one-time QRs and fraud flags replace it).
